One-Pager 1: Mature Small B-Cell Lymphomas vs. Reactive Follicular Hyperplasia (WHO 5th Ed.)

Surgical Path / Heme (50%) + IHC/Flow/FISH (10%) + Day 1 Spot & Day 2 SOE

1. Etiology, Molecular Pathogenesis & Epidemiology: Mature small B-cell neoplasms are clonal proliferations arrested at specific stages of B-cell maturation: (a) Follicular Lymphoma (FL) (peaks at 55–65y; painless generalized lymphadenopathy) is driven by t(14;18)(q32;q21) IGH::BCL2 in 85–90% of nodal cases, placing the anti-apoptotic BCL2 gene under the IGH enhancer alongside chromatin-modifying mutations (KMT2D, CREBBP, EZH2); (b) Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL) (older males 3:1; nodal, splenic, GI lymphomatoid polyposis) is driven by t(11;14)(q13;q32) IGH::CCND1 (>95%), overexpressing Cyclin D1; (c) CLL / SLL (most common adult leukemia in Western populations; median age 70y) harbors del(13q14.3) (miR-15a/16-1), trisomy 12, del(11q) (ATM), or del(17p) (TP53); (d) Extranodal Marginal Zone Lymphoma (MALT) arises from chronic antigenic stimulation (H. pylori in stomach, Chlamydia psittaci in ocular adnexa, Borrelia burgdorferi in skin, Hashimoto thyroiditis, Sjögren sialadenitis) and t(11;18)(q21;q21) BIRC3::MALT1 (predicts resistance to H. pylori antibiotic eradication!); (e) Lymphoplasmacytic Lymphoma (LPL) is driven by MYD88 L265P (>90%) and CXCR4 (30%) mutations with an IgM paraprotein (Waldenström macroglobulinemia); and (f) Hairy Cell Leukemia (HCL) (middle-aged males; splenomegaly, monocytopenia, pancytopenia) is defined by BRAF V600E (~100%).

2. Gross & Microscopic Findings + Differential Diagnosis (+ / -):

  • Gross Findings: Lymphomatous nodes are enlarged, fleshy, tan-white, and homogeneous ("fish-flesh") with capsular/perinodal extension; splenic involvement targets the white pulp (miliary nodules in FL, MCL, MZL, CLL) except Hairy Cell Leukemia, which diffusely expands the splenic red pulp (beefy-red meat appearance with RBC blood lakes and atrophic white pulp).
  • Reactive Follicular Hyperplasia vs. Follicular Lymphoma (+ / -): Reactive Hyperplasia: Preserved nodal architecture and sinuses; variable follicle sizes/shapes with sharp mantle zones; preserved dark/light zone polarization; numerous tingible-body macrophages ("starry-sky"); BCL2 NEGATIVE in germinal center B-cells (internal T-cells and mantle cells are BCL2+); high polarized Ki-67 (>70–90%); polytypic kappa/lambda on flow cytometry. Follicular Lymphoma: Effaced architecture with uniform, closely packed "back-to-back" follicles extending into perinodal fat; attenuated mantle zones; loss of polarization; absent tingible-body macrophages; BCL2 STRONG POSITIVE in germinal center B-cells; CD10 (+), BCL6 (+), LMO2/HGAL (+); low Ki-67 (<20% in low-grade); monotypic surface Ig light chain. WHO 5th Ed. Update & BCL2-Negative Traps: Grades 1, 2, and 3A are now unified as Classic Follicular Lymphoma (cFL), whereas Grade 3B (sheets of centroblasts without centrocytes) is termed Follicular Large B-Cell Lymphoma (FLBL). Pediatric-type FL, primary cutaneous follicle center lymphoma, and high-grade FLBL are frequently BCL2-negative and lack t(14;18)!
