One-Pager 4: Vesiculobullous Skin Diseases (DIF & Salt-Split Skin) & Inflammatory Dermatopathology Patterns

Surgical Path / Dermpath (50%) + Special Studies / DIF (5%) + Day 1 Spot & Day 2 SOE

1. Golden Biopsy Rule (SOE Domain 1 & 4): For any blistering skin disorder, take TWO biopsies: (1) An elliptical or punch biopsy straddling the edge of a fresh intact blister placed in 10% NBF for H&E; and (2) A 3–4 mm punch biopsy of perilesional normal skin (within 1 cm) placed in Michel's Transport Medium for Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF).

Blistering Disease Anatomic Cleavage Level & H&E Morphology Target Autoantigen / Gene Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF) & Salt-Split Skin IIF
Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV) Suprabasal intraepidermal acantholysis; basal keratinocytes remain attached as a 'row of tombstones'; acantholytic Tzanck cells; oral mucosa involved (~90%); Nikolsky (+). Desmoglein 3 (mucosal) & Desmoglein 1 + 3 (mucocutaneous) Intercellular ('chicken-wire') IgG and C3 throughout lower/entire epidermis.
Pemphigus Foliaceus (PF) Subcorneal / Superficial granular layer acantholysis (fragile crusts/scales; NO mucosal involvement). Desmoglein 1 (targeted by Staph exfoliative toxin in SSSS, which is DIF-negative!) Intercellular ('fishnet') IgG and C3 (accentuated in upper epidermis).
Hailey-Hailey vs. Darier Disease • Hailey-Hailey: Full-thickness suprabasal acantholysis ('dilapidated brick wall').
• Darier Disease: Suprabasal clefts + villi + dyskeratosis (corps ronds & grains).		 • Hailey-Hailey: ATP2C1.
• Darier: ATP2A2. (Grover disease is focal transient acantholysis).		 DIF is COMPLETELY NEGATIVE in Hailey-Hailey, Darier, and Grover disease! (Non-autoimmune genetic/transient defects).
Bullous Pemphigoid (BP) Subepidermal tense bullae in elderly (flexural); eosinophil-rich infiltrate; early urticarial phase shows eosinophilic spongiosis. Hemidesmosomal: BP180 (Collagen XVII / NC16A) & BP230 (BPAG1) Linear continuous IgG and/or C3 along the DEJ.
• Salt-Split Skin: Binds to the ROOF (epidermal side)!
Epidermolysis Bullosa Acquisita (EBA) Subepidermal blister (mechanobullous cell-poor scarring on trauma-prone areas). Associated with Crohn disease. Type VII Collagen (anchoring fibrils in sublamina densa) Linear IgG and C3 along the DEJ (u-serrated).
• Salt-Split Skin: Binds to the FLOOR (dermal side)!
Dermatitis Herpetiformis (DH) Subepidermal vesicles with neutrophils and leukocytoclasis at tips of dermal papillae ('papillary neutrophilic microabscesses'); intensely pruritic extensor vesicles. Associated with Celiac Disease. Epidermal Transglutaminase (eTG / TG3); responds to Dapsone & gluten-free diet. Granular (stippled) IgA deposits in tips of dermal papillae! (Distinguishes from linear LABD).
Linear IgA Bullous Dermatosis (LABD) Subepidermal blister with neutrophils aligned in a linear row along the DEJ (mimics DH!). Annular 'string of pearls' blisters in children or adults (Vancomycin-induced!). 97-kDa / 120-kDa shed ectodomain of BP180 (LABD97 / LAD-1) Linear continuous IgA along the DEJ! (Linear vs. the papillary granular IgA of DH).
Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT) Cell-poor subepidermal blister on sun-exposed hands; rigid 'festooning' of dermal papillae; PAS+ thickened papillary vessels; 'Caterpillar bodies' (eosinophilic BM globules in blister roof). Uroporphyrinogen Decarboxylase (UROD) deficiency (HCV, iron overload/HFE). Broad homogenous IgG, IgM, fibrinogen, and C3 in/around superficial dermal vessel walls and along DEJ.

2. Non-Blistering Inflammatory Dermatopathology Quick-Discrimination Pearls:

  • Lichen Planus vs. Lupus Erythematosus (Interface Dermatitis): Lichen Planus has a tight superficial band-like T-cell infiltrate, wedge hypergranulosis, saw-tooth rete ridges, compact orthokeratosis, NO deep perivascular infiltrate, and NO dermal mucin. Lupus Erythematosus (DLE/SLE) shows epidermal atrophy, follicular plugging, superficial AND deep perivascular/periadnexal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, thickened PAS+ basement membrane, positive lupus band test, and abundant interstitial dermal mucin on Alcian blue / Colloidal iron.
  • Granuloma Annulare vs. Necrobiosis Lipoidica: Granuloma Annulare occurs on hands/feet of young patients, upper/mid dermis, with degenerated collagen rich in blue-gray hyaluronic acid mucin (Alcian blue +) without plasma cells. Necrobiosis Lipoidica occurs on anterior shins of diabetics, involves the entire dermis in horizontal 'tiered / layer-cake' bands of eosinophilic acellular collagen, lacks mucin, and contains prominent plasma cells and Touton giant cells.
  • Erythema Nodosum vs. Erythema Induratum (Nodular Vasculitis): Erythema Nodosum is the prototype Septal Panniculitis WITHOUT vasculitis featuring widened septa with giant cells and pathognomonic Miescher radial granulomas. Erythema Induratum (Bazin disease) is a Lobular Panniculitis WITH necrotizing vasculitis, caseous fat necrosis, and tuberculoid granulomas.
