One-Pager 2: Undifferentiated Small Round Cell Sarcomas & Tumors of Uncertain Differentiation (WHO 5th Ed.)

Surgical Path (50%) + Molecular/FISH (10%) + Day 2 SOE

1. WHO 5th Ed. Undifferentiated Small Round Cell Sarcomas: The 5th Edition WHO established four distinct molecularly defined families of round cell sarcomas that must be separated from Alveolar Rhabdomyosarcoma, DSRCT, Mesenchymal Chondrosarcoma, Lymphoblastic Lymphoma, and Neuroblastoma.

2. Key Translocation Sarcomas of Uncertain Differentiation:

  • Synovial Sarcoma (SS18::SSX1/2/4 t(X;18)): Occurs in adolescents/young adults (15–40y) around deep joints. Monophasic: Dense sheets of uniform plump spindle cells with overlapping nuclei, scant cytoplasm, staghorn vessels, and mast cells. Biphasic: Spindle cells + true epithelial glands. IHC: SS18-SSX fusion antibody (+, >95% sensitive, 100% specific), TLE1 (diffuse nuclear +), CD99 (+); CD34 (-).
  • Epithelioid Sarcoma (SMARCB1/INI1 loss): Classic (Distal) type: Young adults, dermis/subcutis of hand/wrist; nodules of plump epithelioid cells surrounding central geographic necrosis, mimicking granuloma annulare or SCC. Proximal type: Deep pelvis/perineum; large rhabdoid cells. IHC: Complete loss of nuclear INI1 (SMARCB1) in >90%, Pan-CK (+), EMA (+), CD34 (+ in 50–60%), ERG (+ in 60%).
  • Alveolar Soft Part Sarcoma (ASPS; ASPSCR1::TFE3): Deep thigh/buttock in young adults. Organoid alveolar packets of large polygonal cells with abundant granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, sinusoidal capillaries, and intracellular PAS-diastase-resistant rod-shaped rhomboid crystals. IHC: Strong nuclear TFE3 (+); Pan-CK (-), EMA (-), S100 (-).
