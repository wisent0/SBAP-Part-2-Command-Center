One-Pager 4: CAP Quality Assurance, Fixatives, Decalcification & SOE Discrepancy Script

Quality & Safety (12% Written) + Day 2 SOE (Manager, Communicator, Professional)

1. Pre-Analytical Fixation & Decalcification Standards (CAP Quality Manual & R1–R5 Curriculum):

Fixative / Solution Mechanism & Penetration Rate Mandatory Clinical Indications Key Artifacts & Pitfalls
10% Neutral Buffered Formalin (NBF; ~4% formaldehyde) Methylene bridge cross-linking of proteins; penetrates ~1 mm/hour; requires 10:1 to 20:1 volume ratio. Routine histology & IHC. ASCO/CAP Breast Rule: Cold ischemia ≤1 hour; fixation duration 6 to 72 hours for ER, PR, and HER2. Unbuffered acidic formalin forms brown-black formalin hematin pigment in blood-rich tissue (mimics melanin/malaria). Cold ischemia >1h degrades antigens & RNA.
2.5% – 3% Buffered Glutaraldehyde Dialdehyde with rapid, strong cross-linking for ultrastructural preservation. Electron Microscopy (EM) — medical renal biopsy, ciliary dyskinesia, muscle/nerve, complex tumors. Poor penetration (dice tissue into 1 mm³ cubes immediately); destroys immunoreactivity for routine IHC.
Michel's Transport Medium / Zeus Fixative Ammonium sulfate buffer (preserves unfixed tissue immunoglobulins without cross-linking). Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF) — medical renal biopsies and blistering skin diseases. Never place DIF biopsy in formalin! Formalin cross-linking masks immunoglobulins/complement and causes autofluorescence.
Absolute (100%) Ethanol / Carnoy's Coagulative/dehydrating non-aqueous fixation. Gout (monosodium urate crystals) & cytology wet-fixed Pap smears (95% ethanol). Water/aqueous formalin dissolves monosodium urate crystals! Fix gout tophi in absolute alcohol to preserve negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals.
EDTA vs. Strong Acid Decalcification EDTA: Chelates calcium gently at neutral pH.
Strong Acid (HCl/Nitric): Rapid acid hydrolysis.		 EDTA is mandatory for bone marrow trephines and any bone/calcified lesion requiring IHC, FISH, PCR, or NGS. Strong acids hydrolyze purine bases and fragment DNA/RNA, causing complete failure of FISH/PCR/NGS and loss of nuclear IHC (ER, PR, Ki-67, TTF1, p53).

2. Ready-to-Recite 4-Domain SOE Oral Script: Suspected Tissue Floater & Amended Report Management:

1. Data-Gathering (Slide & Lab Audit):
Check whether the detached malignant fragment sits in a different focal plane, lacks host tissue reaction, and is absent on deeper levels and the paraffin block face. Audit the grossing bench log, processor basket, embedding station, and microtome water bath for other carcinomas cut the same day.
2. Reasoning & Analytical (STR Workup):
Examine deeper serial sections, re-embed/flip if needed, use lineage IHC, and order PCR-based Short Tandem Repeat (STR) identity testing comparing microdissected DNA from the suspected floater against the patient's verified tissue.
3. Decision-Making (Amended vs. Addendum):
Never sign out a suspected floater without verification. If already signed out (or in a frozen-to-permanent discrepancy), issue a formal Amended (Corrected) Report—not an Addendum, which is only for supplementary data that does not change the diagnosis—preserving the original text and stating the reason for amendment.
4. Professional Attitude & Patient Safety:
Immediately telephone the treating clinician to communicate the amended diagnosis, document the call time and recipient in the report, log the event in the lab's quality assurance/non-conformance system, and perform a root-cause analysis (enforcing clean water baths and wiping forceps between blocks).
